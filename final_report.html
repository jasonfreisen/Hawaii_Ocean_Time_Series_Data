Introduction

The Hawaii Ocean Time‑series (HOT) is a long-term oceanographic research program that organizes monthly cruises to a deep-ocean site, Station ALOHA, north of Oʻahu, Hawaiʻi. The goal of this time-series program is to establish long-term records of physical, chemical and biological conditions for the North Pacific Subtropical Gyre, with the hopes of assessing natural variability and human induced changes. Researches on HOT cruises collect wide ranges of data, such as temperature, salinity, oxygen, carbon, primary productivity, and nutrient fluxes. Measurements are typically also collected at several different depths to compare relative concentrations of nutrients and biomass throughout the water column.

Objectives

In this project, I sought to analyze primary productivity and heterotrophic bacteria measurements collected from the time-series data. Since these measurements were made at several depths during each cruise, I wanted to make visualizations of vertical depth profiles in order to understand which ocean depths had the greatest amount of primary producers vs heterotrophic bacteria.

Data Sources

I used an online data base, Hawaii Ocean Time-series Data Organization and Graphical System (HOT-DOGS), to download time-series data for heterotrophic bacteria and chlorophyll-a concentrations (used as an indicator for primary productivity). Both data of these data sets included variables such as data of collection, collection start and end time, number of bacteria/concentration of chl-a, and depth.Also, in order to create a map of Station ALOHA, I downloaded a depth raster from GMED: Global Marine Environment Datasets for environment visualizations and species distribution modeling.

Data Processing

In order to clean the data into a version better suited for my objectives, I first read the raw data files (.xlsx) into R studio and selected for the columns of interest: date, depth, and bacteria count/chl-a concentration. Next, I cleaned the names and converted the data format from yymmdd to mmddyyy, which was easier to process the data. I then mutated a new column “year” to easily visualize yearly fluctuations instead of monthly. Finally, I added a column with the mean bacteria/chl-a for each year, and grouped by year and depth. My end result clean data only contained three columns: year, depth, and average bacteria/chl-a. This allowed me to effectively create visualizations for average measurements made each year in the time series at different depths.

Main Findings

Based on my final visualizations I made for the data sets, I was able to make a clear vertical profile of both heterotrophic bacteria and chlorophyll-a concentrations. There is a peak in the chlorophyll concentration around 100m depth, where as the heterotrophic bacteria are most prevalent at the surface (0 - 50m depth). While the bacteria and chlorophyll both have yearly variations, there was no clear long-term trend over the ~25 year time frame.

Overall, the greatest number of bacteria was 6.03123^{5}/mL at 45 m depth. The highest concentration of chlorophyll-a was 0.377 mg per cubic meter at 76 m depth
